Your first
four weeks

Twelve sessions. Three a week, with a day off between each. This is the plan we hand people on day one — you can run it here, or anywhere with a barbell and a rack.

Read this bit first. Every session below starts with an empty bar. That is not a warm-up you are allowed to skip and it is not a comment on how strong you are — it is how you find out what today's technique feels like before you commit to a number.

Before your first session

  • Wear anything you can move in. Flat shoes if you own them; running trainers have a squashy heel that makes squatting harder than it needs to be.
  • Bring a water bottle. That's the whole list.
  • Arrive ten minutes early on day one so you're not learning the room and the lifts at the same time.
  • Eat something an hour or two before. Training on an empty stomach is how a first session turns into a bad memory.

The four movements

Everything in the plan is one of these four, or an accessory that props one of them up. Learn the cue and you can self-correct without a coach standing over you.

1 · Squat — knees and hips bend together

Bar on your upper back, feet a bit wider than your hips, toes turned slightly out. Sit down between your feet rather than folding forward over them.

Cue: spread the floor apart with your feet

2 · Hinge — hips travel back, not down

The deadlift and the Romanian deadlift both live here. Your back stays flat, your shins stay nearly vertical, and the movement happens at the hip.

Cue: push your hips back to touch a wall behind you

3 · Press — ribs down before the bar goes up

Overhead or on a bench. Most beginners arch the lower back to get the bar moving; brace your stomach as if expecting a light punch and the arch disappears.

Cue: squeeze your glutes, then press

4 · Pull — elbows lead, shoulders stay down

Rows and, eventually, chin-ups. If you feel it in your neck and upper traps, you are shrugging the weight instead of rowing it.

Cue: put your shoulder blades in your back pockets

How to know a weight is right

Here is the mistake nearly everyone makes: they pick the heaviest weight they can finish, and then wonder why week three hurts. Use this instead.

  • Finish every set feeling like you could have done two or three more reps. Not one. Not zero.
  • If the last rep looks different from the first, the weight is wrong. Take some off — that is not a setback, it is the method.
  • Add weight only when all sets moved cleanly. Roughly 2.5–5 kg on lower-body lifts, 1–2.5 kg on upper.
  • Some days you'll be weaker for no reason you can name. Repeat the previous week rather than forcing it.

Weeks 3 & 4 — adding a little

Same three sessions, same order. Two changes only: one extra set on the first movement of each session, and weight added wherever every set moved cleanly last week. Resist doing more than that — the plan works because it is boring.

The days you don't train

Four days a week you are not in the gym, and they matter more than most beginners expect. You do not need to do anything clever with them.

  • Walk. Twenty to thirty minutes. It does more for how you feel in week two than any stretching routine.
  • Sleep. The training makes you stronger while you're asleep, not while you're lifting.
  • Eat protein at every meal. No weighing, no app. Just make sure it's there.
  • Expect to ache for two or three days after the first session, and much less after the second week. That's normal and it passes.
